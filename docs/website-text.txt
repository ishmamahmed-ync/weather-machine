Act: I · The storm is changing
Title: The Gaps in the Weather Machine
Subtitle: Who can see the storm coming, and who can’t
Text 1: For most of history we guessed at the weather. Now recorded disasters have risen nearly fivefold in fifty years. Yet deaths have fallen almost threefold, thanks to early warning. Our best defence is seeing the storm coming. That depends on a machine.
Source: Disasters and deaths: WMO and UNDRR, *Atlas of Mortality and Economic Losses, 1970–2019* (2021). Tracks: IBTrACS v04r01, NOAA NCEI.

Title: The Gaps

Act: III · The gaps
Title: The Gaps
Text 1: The ocean has gaps too. Floats drift with the currents and miss shelves, enclosed seas and ice. One of these cold spots lies off Mozambique: the Channel gets a third of the global average density of Argo profiles. In March 2019, a cyclone gathered strength there.
Source: Argo GDAC index, downloaded 23 Sep 2026 (profiles per unit area, 35–45°E, 26–11°S). Cold spots: this project’s analysis of that index.

Title: Cell by cell
Text 1: WMO says open plains need about one rain gauge per 575 km², up to about 21 in every one-degree square. Tap any square to see how close it comes. Only 13.4% of the world’s land meets the standard. Europe has 2.4 gauges per 1,000 km². Africa has 0.09.
Source: Su et al., *Nature* 652:119 (2026), Fig. 2c data (CC BY 4.0); WMO-No. 168.

Act: IV · Idai
Title: The storm was seen. The flood was not.
Text 1: Forecasters saw Idai coming five days ahead, and warnings went out. The flood was another matter. Idai moved inland, dumped torrential rain over Zimbabwe, and the rivers carried it back. The Pungwe–Buzi plain became an inland sea. Seeing a storm takes satellites. Knowing what its rain will do takes gauges on the ground.

SLIDE 19   [not-enough]
==============================================================================
Act: VII · Not enough
Title: Adaptation is not enough
Text 1: Every one of those systems works because someone measured something. The poorest countries and small island states deliver 9% of the surface data WMO requires. African weather-balloon reports reaching global models fell by half from 2015 to 2020. Stations get installed, then fall silent, because nobody pays to keep them running.
Bars caption: African weather-balloon reports reaching global models (2015 = 100)
Bar: 2015 | 100
Bar: 2020 | about 50
Source: WMO GBON Baseline 2023 (SOFF); WMO, “Global observing system needs sustainable investments” (2021); stations: GHCN-Daily, 2,119 African stations on 0.5° cells, lit from the first year a cell’s stations report to the last.

SLIDE 20   [rich-maps]
==============================================================================
Act: VII · Not enough
Title: The rich get better maps
Text 1: Where data is rich, technology fills the gaps. Trained on America’s flood maps, an AI model found 11 million people living in flood zones no official map had recorded. It couldn’t be done for Mozambique. With no national flood map to learn from, the plains Idai drowned could not have been flagged this way. And a gap anywhere weakens everyone’s forecast.
Wipe (us): left label: After the model fills the gaps | right label: Official FEMA flood map
Wipe (mozambique): left label: Official national flood map | right label: Model output
Source: Wu, Zhang & Stouffs, *Nature Communications* 17:5983 (2026). The US dots are sampled from a density, not real locations.

SLIDE 21   [fraying]
==============================================================================
Act: VIII · The machine is fraying
Title: The machine is fraying
Text 1: The US, long the machine’s biggest backer, is pulling back: fewer weather balloons, a suspended famine-warning system, exit from 66 international bodies, proposals to cut NOAA by a quarter. Elsewhere, war has destroyed or cut off a quarter of Ukraine’s observing network, and Europe’s weather-satellite agency and the Arctic Council have suspended cooperation with Russia. It is erosion, not collapse. But a lost year of records can never be measured again.

Act: IX · What we do now
Title: The weather machine
Text 1: Climate change threatens every nation’s security, and it hits hardest where the sensors are fewest. The world’s highest court calls it “an existential problem of planetary proportions”. The Cold War moved rivals to build this machine together. This threat is greater.
Text 2: Every forecast on Earth runs on one machine, and it is only as strong as its emptiest places. Fill the gaps in the weather machine.
End card: Weather stations. Rain gauges. River gauges. Radiosondes. Radar.
Source: International Court of Justice, advisory opinion, 23 Jul 2025 (quoted via UNRIC and the UK Human Rights Blog).
