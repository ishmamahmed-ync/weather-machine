[Slide 10 : Mozambique, 2019]
Title: Mozambique, 2019
Description: Mozambique lies downstream of nine international river basins, including the Zambezi and the Limpopo. With over 60% of its population living in low-lying coastal areas, the country is severely exposed to climate change.

[Slide 11 : One season, three storms]
Title: One season, three storms
Description: 21 January 2019: Tropical Storm Desmond brings severe flooding. days later: Cyclone Idai. days after that: Cyclone Kenneth. Mozambique had never been hit by so many cyclones in a single season. Climate change was no longer intangible.

[Slide 12 : The storm was expected. The flood was not.]
Title: The storm was expected. The flood was not.
Description: Forecasters saw Cyclone Idai coming and warned the population. The flooding was another matter. Idai moved inland, dumped torrential rain over Zimbabwe, and the already swollen rivers carried it downstream into Mozambique. The Pungwe–Buzi plain became an inland sea. Foreseeing a storm takes satellites. Knowing its effects takes gauges on the ground.

[Slide 13 : Before and after]
Title: Before and after

[Slide 22 : Some regions do not have the means to build or keep them]
Title: Some regions do not have the means to build or keep them
Description: The poorest countries and small island states provide only 9% of the surface data the WMO needs from them. African weather-balloon reports reaching global models fell by half between 2015 and 2020.

[Slide 23 : Some regions withhold data because of geopolitical tensions]
Title: Some regions withhold data because of geopolitical tensions
Description: 1960: The Indus Waters Treaty is signed. It survives three wars. / 2025: India stops sharing river and flood data with Pakistan. / 2022: The Arctic Council and Europe’s weather-satellite agency suspend cooperation with Russia. / 2024: Data from half the Arctic’s land goes dark.

[Slide 24 : Decline in international cooperation and stewardship]
Title: Decline in international cooperation and stewardship
Description: The USA is sharing less vital atmospheric and ocean data with the world, and leaving international bodies on scientific cooperation around climate change. Europe and Australia are now working to break their reliance on US data, building up their own infrastructure and networks.

[Slide 25 : We have the means to fund planetary defence]
Title: We have the means to fund planetary defence
Description: The UN fund for the poorest countries’ missing weather data is seeking US$400 million. The world collectively spends that on its militaries in just over an hour.
