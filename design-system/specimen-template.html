The Gaps in the Weather Machine

One visual language for the main piece and every prototype around it. Written from what the pages already do, with the drift between them removed. Tokens and components live in design-system/wm.css; the globe's rules in wm.js.

Principles

Six rules the rest follows

  • The night side of the planetDark ground, light data. Colour belongs to the data; text, lines and controls are warm grey.
  • One hue, one meaningAmber is always weather stations, blue is always Argo, on every page. A colour never stands for two things.
  • Say what is shown, and what is notEvery card names its source. Anything not real says illustrative where it appears. Keys say what a layer is not.
  • Lines before boxesCards over the globe are the only boxes. Fields are underlines, buttons are outlines. No drop shadows.
  • The globe is always thereWords on the left, globe on the right. When a big image takes the stage, the globe minimises to the top-right corner. It never leaves.
  • Every image is framedA white frame round every photograph: 16:9 inside cards, 4:3 for big images beside the text. Every image has a credit.

The globe

Real data, drawn with wm.js

  • Orthographic, rotation negated onceStore real [lon, lat]; WM.rotateFor() negates it for d3. Drag to turn.
  • Density gridsAdditive squares, density as brightness. WM.gridAlpha(n, max): power .85, floor .13, gain 7.
  • DotsRadius grows with the fourth root of zoom, WM.dotRadius().
  • MarkersRing, hover fill, selected solid with a halo; WM.drawMarker(); 14px hit radius.
  • Graticule and rim10° at 0.5px, 15% white; rim 1px, 60% white. Shaded relief on the live pages.
  • Weather stations, 0.5° cells (land standard)Argo profiles, 1° cells (ocean standard)Markers: rest, hover, selected
